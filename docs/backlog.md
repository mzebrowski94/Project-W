# Engineering backlog

Structural work we decided to defer, each with the reason it waited and what should bring it back. Not
gameplay: a stand-in for a mechanic belongs in `design/alpha-phase.md` (*Still scaffolding*), and an open
design question in `design/README.md`.

An entry is removed when it is done, or rejected in an ADR. Taking one on is a normal session: agree, plan,
tests first. Origin: the `sim` review of 2026-09-26/27, the `net` review and the new-systems review; item
numbers in brackets refer to those reports, which are not kept in the repository.

## Performance

| Item | Why it waited | Bring it back when |
|---|---|---|
| **One shared neighbour index** [B3, B4]. `SpatialGrid` is built twice per tick: by `ConstraintSolver` for pairs, and by `AvoidanceSystem` for its hazard queries. Everything else that looks for things nearby still scans a family: `DodgeSystem` (dodgers × shots), `TrooperFight`, `TurretSystem`, `MineSystem`, `BlastSystem`, `PickupSystem`, `Rouse`. The fix is one index owned by `Simulation`, rebuilt at the top of the tick, `@NotState`, with ties broken on spawn order. | At 800 entities a step is 4 ms of a 16.7 ms tick. Avoidance is 61% of that; the rest is linear and small (`performance.md`: theoretical at this scale). | The benchmark's 800 crowd passes 8 ms, or one of those systems shows up in a profile. |
| **Performance guard is opt-in** (`PWT_BENCH=1`, about 25 s). Its time and allocation checks do not run on every verify. | Wall time is noisy and needs a warm JIT; 25 s is a lot to add to a 3 s verify. | A regression slips through that the guard would have caught, or CI appears. Only the allocation check could move into the default run. |
| **The checksum runs on the game thread** [net review P2]. Every `checksumTicks` each peer snapshots and hashes its world in `PeerSession.step`: 0.05-1.09 ms per second today (`Adventure`, 279 entities, 138 KB), about two steps' worth. | Once a second and within budget. | Worlds past ~1000 entities, or a once-a-second frame spike shows up online. Hash incrementally, or hash on another thread from the snapshot bytes. |
| **No opt-in bench for the network** [net review E5]. The stage-1 regression test covers 8 seats at 120 ms, but the full table (ticks behind, lead and bundle bytes per tick by seats × latency) exists only as a deleted probe. | The default test catches the defect it was built for. | The next change to how bundles or input are sent: build it behind `PWT_NETBENCH=1` and list it in `workflow.md`. |

## Architecture

| Item | Why it waited | Bring it back when |
|---|---|---|
| **Definitions copied into components** [C4]. `Spawner.applyStrain` copies about 60 values from a `UnitDef` into 32 components per grunt, through positional setters (`Perception.set`, `Leap.set`). Every snapshot and hash carries every tunable of every entity. The alternative: components hold state only and tunables are read through the kind (`config.unit(strain.kind).leap()`), so evolving just changes the kind. Migrate one component at a time. | A large mechanical change. Decided "skip for now" in stage 5. | Snapshot size matters online (drop-in over a slow link), or a positional setter misplaces a value. |
| **Presentation still in the deterministic state** [D3]. Only `Facing` was pulled out. `AppearanceSystem` (about 400 lines) still runs every tick on every peer, and `Appearance`, `Shake` and `Focus` are in the snapshot and the hash, so a cosmetic change is a simulation change. The alternative: the same pure function in `sim`, run by the client after `step`, not a system and not snapshotted. The G0003 contract does not change. | It needs a design call on what the client may compute. The part the rules depended on (`Facing`) is already out. | An online desync is traced to a cosmetic field, or snapshots need to shrink. |
| **ADR 0001 (Ashley): its revisit trigger is out of date** [D2]. It says "tens of thousands of entities". The real cost is working around Ashley: the spawn-order shim (ADR 0013), pooled references (solved by handles, ADR 0016), the fixed-composition rule that forces a superset of about 32 components with `enabled` flags on every alien, and reflection for the codec. | Changing a structural ADR is Mateusz's decision. Nothing forces a migration now. | Suggested new trigger: a second reflection shim into Ashley, or a feature blocked by the composition rule. |
| **`WorldCodec` still writes `Entity` fields** as a place in spawn order, although `HandleTest` ensures no component has one any more. | Dead code costs nothing yet. | Next time the codec is touched: delete the path and narrow `ArchitectureTest.componentFieldsAreWhatASnapshotCanWrite`. |
| **`Simulation`'s surface** [C6]: about 20 pass-through `spawnX` methods and a public `engine()`, so `core` can technically write the world. | Low priority; `UiArchitectureTest` already stops `ui` writing a component. | Give tests and scenarios one `spawner()` accessor, and give `core`/`ui` a read-only view. |
| **Trooper component split** [C3]. The 40 fields of `Trooper` would become orders / sight / walk-and-look / dues. | After the behaviour split every field has a visible owner. The split would cost 3-4 pooled components and about 90 rewritten accesses for little gain (stage 4). | The implicit mode-plus-booleans state produces a bug. |
| **One ranking of what the action key offers** [architecture-review 10]. `Reach.nearest` finds the nearest casualty, empty gun or shut crate, but which of the three wins is hand-written in `ActionSystem.update`, two comparisons and a tie rule (G0021). | Three interactables are two comparisons; a ranking query would be a layer over them. | A fourth thing the action key can be done to. |
| **Held input as "same as last tick"** [net review X2]. One bit per present slot, 17 bytes only when the input changed; lossless. An 8-seat bundle of steady players drops from 152 to ~12 bytes. | After stage 1's resend fix a far peer costs ~3 bundles a tick (466 B at 8 seats); no playtest has hit a bandwidth limit. A wire change. | Host upload shows in `cat=stats` as a limit, a session goes past 4 machines over the internet, or rollback arrives (it resends input far more often). |
| **`ScenarioWorlds` into `net`** [net review X3], parsing with `SimConfig.parse` and logging to the `NetLog`; `TestWorlds` goes away and `SessionHarness` ships real config text, so the session tests cover the "host ships its config" path (today only `core` does). | Optional; the path is tested in `core`. | A config-shipping bug that `net`'s tests did not catch. |
| **A joiner whose snapshot is too big waits forever, told nothing** [net review D3]. Since stage 1 the host logs `event=snapshot-too-big` instead of crashing, but the sequencer never asks again and the joiner's screen says only that it is joining. | 138 KB is the largest world today against an 8 MB limit. | A world within 10x of `maxMessageBytes`: refuse the joiner with a reason (a `REFUSED` it can show), or send the snapshot in parts. |
| **Local keys inside the world config.** `sim.seed` (the session's seed overrides it) and `sim.maxTicksPerFrame` (read only by the offline `FixedStepClock`) are **local config** in `sim/*.properties`, so a host ships them and no peer reads them. Their file comments say so. | Two keys; moving them reshapes `SimConfig` and a key name for nothing a player feels. | A third local key turns up in `sim/*.properties`: move them all out of the world config. |
| **LAN discovery reads the network adapters once, when hosting starts** (`LanAddresses.beaconTargets`, `reachable`). An adapter that comes up later - Hamachi connecting after the host started - is never announced to, and every IPv4 adapter (VirtualBox, WSL) is read out in the invitation line. | Found while reading, never hit: Hamachi is normally up before the game starts. | A friend on Hamachi cannot see a host that can see him, or the invitation line grows too long to read out. |
| **`Msg.PROTOCOL` is not kept** [new-systems V10]. It stayed 3 when `Bundle`'s layout changed in `77d5687` (crate events); nothing ties a wire change to a bump. | Harmless: `BuildId` hashes the sources of `map`, `sim` and `net` and refuses any other build first, so a peer on a different layout never gets as far as a bundle. | Two different builds must play together (a patch released beside the old one): pin the layout with golden bytes per message type, and bump on a change. |
| **A power budget per upgrade** (upgrades plan, source 3): a number per card that a draft's deal balances against. | Twelve player upgrades; weights and tiers are enough. | Fifteen or more upgrades, or a playtest where one draft offers three cards of very different worth. |
| **Fixture definitions are positional.** `AlphaWorlds.config()` writes every gun and pickup out as a full `new WeaponDef(...)` (27 arguments) or `new PickupDef(...)`, so a field added to a def is an edit to every literal. A kind the fixture leaves out already takes the shipped definition (`onShipped`, 2026-10-03), so only the new-field case remains. The alternative: each literal as the shipped def with the numbers a test reads changed through `@With`, so a new field arrives with its shipped value. | About 2000 lines of churn, and nothing broken. | The next field added to `WeaponDef` or `PickupDef`: convert that def's literals in the same session. |
| **Turret, mine and nest are one of a kind each.** Each has its own config record and its own `Spawner.spawnX`, not a kind enum, a `Defs` table and a block per kind as strains, guns and pickups have (G0008). A second kind of one is a structural change today. | Nothing has a second kind; the general shape waits for its second user (CLAUDE.md, *Small patch, right shape*). | A second kind of turret, mine or nest is named: give that family a kind enum and a `Defs` table, with today's config as its first block. |
| **One solidity for moving, shooting and seeing** (G0091, provisional, for review). `Terrain.passable`, read through `Solid`, answers the solver's clamp, a shot's flight, a blast's line, perception's clear line and a shooter's choice of target alike - so the caldera's lava stops shots as rock does, and nobody fights across a pool. The alternative: a second question on `Terrain` ("blocks a line") answered through `Solid` beside `passableAtTile`, and every line-walk (`TileMap.clearLine`/`passableFraction`, `ProjectileBoundsSystem`, `Sight`) asked it while the solver and the flow field keep asking the first. | One terrain wants it, and it threads through every line-walk in `sim` and `map`; lava as solid ground plays without it. | A second terrain wants to block movement and not shots (water, a chasm), or a playtest asks to shoot across a pool. |
