# G0013: The clinch is what a parry earns, not what a kill costs

**Trigger.** Melee kills, the clinch, or the stun a kill applies.

**Enforced by.** Nothing mechanical - caught by reading.

**Constraint.** A melee weapon spends its lock only on a target that had **already committed to
attacking** - wound up to leap, in the air, or between the leaps of a sequence. Kill something that
was merely walking towards you and the sweep is a swat: the thing dies, and the commander keeps his
feet and pays nothing but the cooldown.

Two consequences worth naming:

- **A lethal exchange ends with the two of them touching.** A sweep reaches further than the
  commander's own body, so a leap answered at full stretch dies a stride short of him - and a killing
  blow throws nothing and a corpse is immovable, so nothing would close that stride. The body is
  pulled the last stride at the moment of death. Without it the beat reads as him flinching at thin
  air rather than as a clinch, which is what made this record necessary.
- **Only a *fatal* lock outranks being dead on screen.** Something swatted while it walked is stunned
  as it dies and reads as dying, because there is nothing for the player to be in suspense about.

**Scope.** Melee. An enemy's claws still lock both parties on any kill, because the thing that dies
there is the commander and that beat is the drama the whole exchange exists for. The switch is a flag
on the attack, so a future weapon can go either way.

**DO NOT BUILD** - rejected, and likely to be re-proposed.

**No:** *A kill always clinches, whatever it killed.* The rule this replaces, and the simpler sentence. It
makes the safest thing on the field the most expensive one to kill - clearing a wandering grunt roots
you for a second while the pack that has noticed you closes - so the player learns not to swing at
anything that is not already in the air, which is the opposite of the weapon reading as usable.

**No:** *Give the sweep the commander's own reach, so a kill always lands at touching distance and nothing has
to be pulled.* It works, and it deletes the parry: the sweep would have to land after the leap has
already connected. Reach and clinch distance are different numbers for a reason.

**No:** *Let the corpse keep its momentum and slide the last stride instead of being placed.* More physical,
and it needs a corpse that is immovable to nothing except this - a second rule in the solver for one
frame of one mechanic.

**Revisit when** a melee weapon exists that is meant to root the commander on every kill - a heavy one
whose whole cost is commitment. That is a different flag on the same attack, not an amendment here.
