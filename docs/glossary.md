# Glossary

Only terms whose meaning is not obvious from the name. Add one in the same commit that introduces
it. Terms marked *(source)* come from *Triumph! War 2099*.

Grouped by subsystem, alphabetical within each group. The grouping is for **spotting a word we already
have**: near-duplicates always come from the same subsystem, and one concept gets one word everywhere.
Before adding a term, read its section end to end - not just search for the name you had in mind.

A term that could sit in two sections goes where a reader would look for it, not where the code puts it:
*dodge* is under Fighting although `DodgeSystem` is part of the steering pipeline.

### Units and sides

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ant** | The strain in two segments (G0045): an **armoured** head that turns a bullet, and a leap that **grips** a man and carries him off rather than killing him. The problem it sets is where you stand - you get round it, or put a fist in its face. Drawn as two circles inside the one body, head darker. |
| **boss** | The one at the end of a level: a **strain** grown too big to walk, **rooted** among **nests**, shooting a **barrage**. The first is the boss grunt, at the east end of Adventure 2's keep, kept off the field by a **lair** (G0048). A boss is its numbers - nothing in the code knows it is one. |
| **rooted** | A **strain** whose speed is zero: it never walks, the solver pushes whatever it meets out of its way rather than it out of theirs, and a blow that would throw anything else throws nothing (G0048). The **boss** is the only one. |
| **brute** | A heavy enemy: slower, tougher, and able to survive a hand strike. Carries everything an ordinary enemy carries, so only spawning and drawing tell them apart. |
| **commander** *(source)* | The unit a player directly controls. There may be up to `Players.MAX`, one per **slot**. |
| **seat** | A device's place on one machine, and putting it there. The keyboard's is zero; a pad is seated in the lowest free one the first time it is seen and keeps it while it is unplugged, because a pulled cable is almost always followed by the pad coming back (G0028). A machine has at most four. Not a **slot**: offline the two are the same number, and online the **host** maps each **peer**'s seats to slots (G0038). |
| **faction** | Which side an entity is on. Damage only ever crosses sides. Scenery is on nobody's. |
| **frog** | The hopping strain: green, middle-ranged, and a different problem from a spitter. What it throws does nothing on its own - the **slick** it leaves is the weapon - so what it makes you read is the ground rather than the creature. It gets about by **hopping**, and it gives up on you far sooner than anything else on the field, which makes backing off a real answer to one. |
| **grunt** | The basic enemy strain. *Enemy* stays the umbrella word for everything on the alien side; *grunt* is the specific creature. |
| **obstacle** | A static entity that blocks movement and projectiles. An entity like any other, not terrain data. |
| **runner** | The fast strain: smaller, thinner, and it notices the commander from most of the way across a level. What makes it a different problem from a grunt is its line rather than its speed - it **weaves** as it closes and **dodges** shots it sees coming, so it is a target you have to lead rather than one you have to out-run. It dashes the last of the distance as a grunt does. |
| **spitter** | The shooting strain: purple, fragile, and it holds its ground at a distance instead of closing. A different problem from the two above - one you have to go and solve rather than one that comes to you. |
| **slot** | A player in the simulation, as a number below `Players.MAX`. The whole of what ties a player to a commander: offline it is the same number as the **seat** of the device driving it, online the **host** hands it out (G0038); one input per slot arrives each tick, and a commander killed and brought back is the same player because the slot comes back with him. It is also the tie-break when two commanders order one trooper at once - the higher slot is heard last (G0028). |
| **squad** *(source)* | The group of troopers following the commanders. One squad, shared: an order from any commander overwrites an order from another, and a trooper follows the man who told it to. |
| **strain** | The form a unit currently has. Grunt, brute and whatever they grow into are strains of one thing, not different classes of entity: a strain is an enum on a component and a table of numbers, and changing it is a field write. |
| **trooper** | One AI-controlled member of a squad. |
| **turret** | A gun on a mount that a man gets on rather than picks up (G0024). It stays where it is put - though it can be **hauled** like a barrel while nobody is on it - fires far faster and far lighter than anything carried, and has to be swung onto a bearing instead of pointed. Well over three times a trooper's health, and it goes off when it is destroyed. Drawn square, because it is the one thing on the field that is equipment rather than a creature. |

### Growth and pickups

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **barrel** | An explosive barrel: a pickup the commander is offered nothing by and leaves standing. The one **shunned** pickup, so the aliens steer round it and only **bite** into one on a roll. What one is worth to them depends on what reached it: something that bites sets it off in its own face, and something that can **swallow** it comes away with a **charged** gun instead (G0022). |
| **bite** | A creature setting off something it should have left alone. Rolled while it is actually touching the thing, so avoidance keeping it away is most of what makes a bite rare - and the blast is far smaller than a shot's, because the biter had its mouth on it. |
| **charge** | How full something is, as a fraction the simulation publishes for the renderer - a unit part-way to evolving, a control point being taken, a man being put back on his feet. One tell, drawn the same way wherever it appears; the one that is good news names a colour of its own so it cannot be mistaken for a clock running out. |
| **charged** | Carrying a barrel rather than having been blown up by one. A strain that names a charged gun **swallows** an explosive it reaches and fires shots that go off where they land (G0022). |
| **evolve** | A unit changing strain. One way, and it arrives at the new strain's full health - a unit that has just become something else arrives whole. |
| **feed** | What a unit has eaten towards its next evolution, as a count. Crossing its strain's threshold evolves it; an item that names a strain outright skips the count entirely. |
| **grant** | What a pickup gives one side for taking it. Every pickup carries two - one per side - and either may be nothing, which is what makes that side walk over it and leave it. |
| **fuse** | The flashing on a **mine** that has started. A triangle wave off the mine's own clock whose period shortens as it runs out, drawn as a near-black core - the only thing on the **charge** channel darker than what it is drawn on, because a thing about to go off must not read as a thing filling up. What the player reads is the quickening, never a number (pillar 1). |
| **mine** | The **barrel**'s opposite number: something small lying in the open that starts its **fuse** when the other side comes near, and explodes by **dying** like everything else that explodes (G0018). **Only the other side arms one** - a commander and his troopers walk straight over them - but the blast is on nobody's side, so laying one badly is still his own doing. It can be **hauled** exactly as a barrel can, which is most of what one is worth: they are laid before a fight rather than during it. Solid, and the one body in the game that is deliberately *not* steered around. |
| **pickup** | Anything lying on the field that the first thing to touch it takes. Not the player's alone: what it does depends on which side took it, and one item can be a weapon to the commander and a meal to what is chasing him. |
| **shunned** | A pickup that is steered around rather than walked over, because what it hands whoever takes it is an explosion. Derived from the grant rather than declared beside it, so the two can never disagree. The barrel is the only one - and it is shunned **by whatever cannot use one**: anything that can **swallow** an explosive crosses the ground for it like any other food (G0022). |
| **swallow** | Taking an explosive whole instead of setting it off. No blast at all: the creature is carrying it now. |
| **rabbit** | Alien food that runs away. A pickup in every way that matters - claimed on contact, granted from the same table - but it walks, it dodges, and it can be shot. The commander is offered nothing by one, so his only interest is denying it. |
| **reinforcement** *(source)* | A pickup that adds troopers to the squad - three of them, as the shipped one is. Taking one lands a count on the level entity, which `ReinforcementSystem` turns into men standing at the **rally point**, one a tick, up to the squad's maximum; past it they go into the **reserve**, and past the reserve's most they are wasted (G0055). A level lays two or three beside every control point. |
| **reserve** | The men a side has off the field (G0055): `Level.reserve`, shown on the HUD beside the men on it. Every man who comes onto the field after the first tick is one of them - a **muster**'s trooper, or a commander coming back. Starts at `sim.reserve.start`, never holds more than `sim.reserve.max`. Every commander out with nobody in reserve loses the level. Not the squad's headroom, which is room on the field. |
| **muster** | The reserve calling men onto the field on a clock (G0055): every `sim.reserve.musterSeconds`, `musterMin` to `musterMax` of them dealt from a bag, as many as the field has room for, at the **rally point**. A full field leaves them in reserve. |
| **rally point** | Where a man comes onto the field after the first tick - a muster's, a crate's, a returning commander (G0055): the spawn area until a point is taken, the point taken last after that. `RallyPoint`. Not the **rally radius**, which is about a squad following a commander between points. |
| **lost** *(level)* | Every commander out, somebody waiting to come back, and nobody in reserve (G0055). `Level.failed`, latched; the goal line says MISSION FAILED. The opposite of *complete*, which is recomputed every tick. |
| **shine** | The slow pulse on a pickup worth crossing the ground for. Declared per kind as `shineSeconds`, zero meaning it does not shine, and published on the charge channel as `Look.GLEAM` so the renderer draws it without being told what the thing is. |
| **weapons cache** | A crate with a gun in it: the player's alone, and one **kind** per gun so which one it is can be read before he has walked to it - a **sealed** one shows its gun before anybody can reach it (G0050). Picking one up replaces what he was carrying; nothing about it survives his death. |
| **sealed cache** | A **weapons cache** whose gun is dealt from the level's **armoury deck** the first time a commander comes near, and shown from then on. Nobody can take one while it is sealed (G0050). |
| **supply drop** | A crate of men lying on the field: three **reinforcements** to whoever on the player's side reaches it, and nothing at all to the other side, which walks over one and leaves it standing. Renamed from *frozen trooper* on 2026-09-18, when it stopped being a mouthful to the aliens - the old name described a body being contested, and it is now kit. |
| **medkit** | A pickup that clears every **wound**. Worth nothing to a man without one, so he walks over it, and scenery to the aliens and to weapons (G0053). |

### Fighting

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **armour** | An arc round a creature's facing where gunfire **glances off**: a shot whose flight comes at it from inside the arc lands nothing and is spent. Judged by the side the shot arrives from, never by where on the body it touched, and against gunfire only - a **strike**, a **blast** and **fire** land wherever they land (G0045). Only the **ant**'s head has any. |
| **blast** | The circle of ground an explosion makes lethal, for a moment. One circle, uniformly fatal inside it and harmless outside it, on nobody's side, and stopped by rock (G0018). It catches what is standing in it on the tick it appears and nothing that walks in afterwards, so the rest of it is only the flash. Everything that explodes does so by **dying**, and a blast is what the death leaves. |
| **bleed-out** | The window a casualty has before he is gone. It stops while he is being hauled, so the ground you cross carrying him is free; what it costs is the work at the far end. Published as a fraction on the `charge` channel, because a rescue the player cannot time is a rescue he cannot decide to attempt. |
| **casualty** | A man shot or blasted off his feet but not dead: he lies where he fell with a **bleed-out** running, and somebody who reaches him in time can **stabilise** him. He takes no **wound** while he lies there, but anything that could have put him down or killed him standing finishes him (G0053). **The commander can be one**, now that the squad comes for him (G0023). Nothing on the alien side can, because nothing on that side would come and get it (G0020). Distinct from *going down*, which everywhere else in this codebase means dying. |
| **pierce** | A shot that passes through anything alive rather than stopping at the first of it. Declared as a number on the gun - the least time between the blows one shot lands - and zero on every gun that stops where it hits. It is stopped by **scenery** alone, which is the rule a thrown grenade has followed since G0019, and rock stops it the way rock stops any shot. What it rewards is a line rather than a target. **That number is a time, so on the shot it is a length of flight** - speed times interval - during which the shot passes through everything and lands nothing. It only means "one blow per body" on a gun slow enough that the length is shorter than one body: the railgun's is 4.25 units, and the laser's was nineteen, which is why the laser does not pierce. |
| **clinch** | How a lethal exchange ends: both parties stopped dead and held still, the body immovable, and the last stride closed so the two finish touching. It carries no knockback decay - a killing blow throws nothing, so there is nothing to bleed off, and carrying one let the survivor coast out from under the body. A melee kill earns it only against something already committed to attacking; a swat on something walking is not a clinch and costs the commander nothing (G0013). |
| **slick** | One circle of ground a **venom** shot has ruined. A handful of overlapping ones - a **spatter** - is what a burst leaves, and each is an ordinary body carrying an ordinary blow, so who it catches is the contact rule the game already has. Two things make it not a **blast**: it is a *place* rather than an event, so it catches whatever walks into it for as long as it lasts; and **it takes a side**, so venom is a problem for the player's men and not for the frogs that spat it. It **wounds** a man and shoves him off it, as gunfire wounds him (G0053). |
| **spatter** | The handful of overlapping **slicks** one burst leaves. Not a thing in its own right - there is no entity for it, only the circles - which is why the shape is a number on the gun rather than anywhere in a system. |
| **venom** | What a frog fires. A fat, slow shot with a reach, and **its own damage is zero**: what it does to you is the **spatter** it leaves where it stops. A miss bursts short rather than flying on, which is what makes standing in the wrong place the mistake. |
| **contact** | An overlapping pair, reported once per tick by the constraint solver. What it *means* is the collision system's business. |
| **decisive** | What a blow is when it empties a health bar - which only a thing with health has. A man reads the blow's **harm** instead, declared by the source rather than derived from the amount: gunfire and a grenade wound, other blasts put him down, claws and sweeps and leaps kill (G0020, G0053). The worst harm wins when two hits combine. |
| **hit point** | One point of health or damage, always a whole number (G0054). Where a percentage would make a fraction - the swarm's health, an upgraded gun - it is rounded there, once: nearest, halves up, never to zero. A man has none; he has **wounds**. |
| **death throes** | The window between a killing blow and the body being removed. Every unit has them. The body is scenery for their duration - solid, immovable, and it stops bullets. Where the dying animation will play; a commander's respawn countdown starts after them, not during. |
| **fire** | Ground an explosion has left burning. One circle of it is a **slick** in every way that matters - a place rather than an event, an ordinary body carrying an ordinary blow, and it **wounds** a man and shoves him off it (G0053) - declared differently in three (G0035): it is on **nobody's side**, as the blast that left it was, so it catches the commander as readily as what he threw it at; it is **steered around**, where venom is walked through; and it **swells and collapses**, so the circle that burns you is the one you can see at that instant rather than a fixed ring. It comes out of the blast **dying**, which is why the flamethrower leaves none - it leaves no blast. |
| **dodge** | A sidestep away from an incoming shot. Perpendicular to the shot, to the side the creature is already on, the same every time, so it can be learned and led (G0012). Bounded by a commit window, which is what makes the answer to a dodge a second shot. |
| **grip** | What a leap that does not bite does instead: it takes hold of the man it lands on and **carries** him away from whoever is nearest him, with a clock on him drawn on his **charge** ring. At the end of it, the creature's own blow. Killing the creature is the only way out, and a man in its jaws is **gripped**: he drives nothing and fires nothing, and may still **struggle** (G0045). Not *grab*, not *carry* on its own - *carry* is the last stride of a **clinch**. |
| **grenade** | What the commander throws. It travels along his aim **direction** for a fixed distance, comes off rock and scenery without losing speed, passes through anything alive, and leaves a blast where the distance runs out (G0019). The reach is the same every throw, so a wall changes where one ends up rather than how far it went. |
| **hesitation** | The pause a unit takes before the first leap of a sequence. The tell: the only part of a leap the player gets to answer. Drawn from the seeded `Rng`, so it varies between leaps and not between runs. |
| **knockback** | The velocity a landed blow gives a target **that survives it**, bleeding off over the stun. A blow that kills throws nothing - the body stops where it was struck. |
| **leap** | A unit throwing itself at the commander to close the last of the distance. Committed, telegraphed, and aimed once at launch - never steered. |
| **lock** | The stun a *fatal* blow puts on both parties. What turns a kill into an exchange rather than a deletion. |
| **projectile** | A fired entity that travels and can hit something. Always simulated; there is no hitscan. |
| **recovery** | The window after a staggering hit during which an alien takes no further contact damage. A man's is **grace** (G0053). |
| **heat** | How hot a gun has become, as a fraction the simulation publishes for the renderer. It fills with every shot and comes off at a rate, always - a gun nobody is holding cools exactly as fast as one somebody is. The one bar in the game that fills up because of what the player **did** rather than something being done to him, which is why it is drawn as an arc over the holder rather than on the **charge** channel (G0025). |
| **overheat** | A gun that has filled its **heat** bar and stopped itself. It stays stopped until the bar has fallen well below full, not merely below the top - so running one into the stop costs seconds rather than a stutter. |
| **volley** | Everything one pull of a trigger puts in the air. One for most guns; a **scattergun** names more, and they leave together inside the gun's own **spread**. One place decides it for every shooter there is, so what a pull does is a property of the gun and never of who is holding it. |
| **barrage** | A **boss**'s way of shooting: a cycle of **patterns**, each shown by a wind-up and followed by a rest, worked through for as long as it has somebody to shoot at. Counted, never rolled, so it can be learnt (G0048). Not a **volley** - a volley is one pull of one trigger. |
| **pattern** | One step of a **barrage**: a number of **salvos** at an interval, each turned a little from the last. A ring, a fan, a spiral and a stream are all one pattern with different numbers. |
| **salvo** | Every shot of one **pattern** that leaves at once, spread across its arc - all the way round for a ring, a few degrees for a stream. |
| **spew** | What the boss grunt throws in its patterns: slower than a **spit** and half as hard, because it comes twenty at a time, and it fades out a short way from him, so his fight has an edge. |
| **scattergun** | A gun that covers ground rather than a point: several **pellets** per **volley**, thrown into a cone of its own. Two exist - the **flamethrower** and the **shotgun** - and they are the same trade at different distances. |
| **pellet** | One shot of a **volley**. Not a round: what makes a scattergun a scattergun is that a pellet on its own is worth little and six of them at arm's length are worth everything. |
| **flight** | How far a shot travels before it goes out, as a distance on the gun. Zero means until it hits something or leaves the level, which is what almost every gun says. **A property of the shot and never of the aim** - a grenade has had one since G0019 - so it is what lets a gun be short-ranged without touching the rule that aim is a direction. |
| **flamethrower** | The short gun. Four fat, short-lived **pellets** per pull into a wide cone, so it covers the ground right in front of him and nothing past it. It runs hot, which is the whole of what stops it being the answer to everything: it is the gun most likely to be held down. |
| **shotgun** | The other short gun, and the opposite rhythm. Six **pellets** thrown wide, slowly: all of them kill a grunt outright and one or two at range annoy it, so how much of a pull lands is entirely how close he was willing to get. It stays cold; what it costs is the wait. |
| **laser** | The beam: a stream of very small, very fast shots with a reach, forty a second, each worth almost nothing, each **drawn as a line back to the muzzle it came from** so the stream reads as one unbroken beam that grows out of the gun and **stops dead on the first thing it touches**. It is still not a hitscan - every point on that line is on the path a shot actually took, and the line ends where the shots do. It **slows** what it lands on, it slows *him* while he holds it down, and it **bloats** what it has killed; all of it comes out of the same **heat** bar, which is the whole of what makes it a resource rather than a default. About four seconds of trigger, then nearly two of nothing: the hottest gun a man can carry, by a long way, and the only one worth pointing at something already dead. |
| **slow** | Being robbed of some of your own speed. Not a **stun**: a slowed unit goes on doing everything it was doing and simply gets less of the speed it asked for. It comes from places that are one answer - something **done** to it, which lives on a component and runs out; the **ground** it is standing on, which is asked of the map and never stored; and the gun it is holding down, because a beam costs the man holding it as well as the thing it is on - and they multiply. It scales a course and never turns one, so a slowed thing walks exactly where it meant to and takes longer about it. |
| **bloat** | What sustained **laser** on a thing does to it: a bright ring growing out from the body's own edge, and **the body itself is never touched**. The charge builds on the living too, at a fraction of the rate, so burning something down leaves its corpse *part* of the way there and a decision still to make about it; **a thing being charged does not settle at all**, so the gun is never fighting the clock; only something already **dying** bursts, because a gun that killed by inflation would make health beside the point. A bigger body takes proportionally longer and leaves a proportionally wider circle, off one reference radius rather than a number per strain. Full, it goes off by **dying**, through the one death reaction (G0018), and it is the only explosion in the game that leaves **no fire** - the whole point of blowing a body in a doorway is the doorway. Let go and the charge falls away; while any of it is left the corpse waits, so half a heat bar spent on one is not half a bar lost. |
| **muzzle** | Where a shot leaves a gun. A number on the gun rather than on whatever is holding it: a gun that names an offset has two of them, fires from each in turn, and is therefore a twin mount without anything growing a second firing path. Never *barrel* - that word is taken by the explosive. |
| **stagger** | The stun a *survived* blow puts on the one that took it, and only on that one. A shove, not a clinch: whoever landed it keeps fighting. |
| **spit** | What a spitter fires. Slow in the air on purpose, and thrown inside a narrow cone rather than along a line - so a shot meant for the commander reaches the men standing beside him, which is most of what makes a firing line hurt a squad. |
| **attack arc** | How wide an arc in front of a creature its claws reach into, centred on the way it is going. Below all round on purpose: walking into the back of something that had not turned round used to kill outright, which read as the game cheating rather than as a mistake. Generous, though - a lunging creature is not precise, and something standing still reaches all round because it can turn on the spot. |
| **spread** | The width of the cone a shot may leave in, centred on the aim. Applied by turning the shot, never by nudging where it was aimed: turning keeps every shot the same length and the cone the same width at every distance. It **settles**: the first shot is the wild one. |
| **settle** | A shooter walking its aim in. Every shot it lands on the same target narrows its **spread** towards the floor its strain declares, and being close narrows it further and shortens the wait. Losing the target throws all of it away, which is why breaking a shooter's line beats trading with it. |
| **stabilise** | Putting a casualty back on his feet. It needs somebody within arm's length and it takes time standing still; the commander holds his action key for it, and a trooper does it off its own judgement (G0023). Letting go loses the work rather than banking it. He gets up hurt and briefly protected, and a man who has been **hauled** takes longer. |
| **strike** | One swing of a melee weapon, as an entity in the world with a body that sweeps an arc (G0005). Never an overlap query. |
| **struggle** | A **gripped** man swinging at what holds him. Not a mechanic of its own - it is a **strike** like any other, landing on the creature like anybody else's - so what frees him is that it dies. The commander struggles on his melee key; a trooper on `AiChance.STRUGGLE`, slowly on purpose. |
| **stun** | A window during which an entity cannot act - no control, no chase, no leap, no blow. It can still be moved and still be shoved; that is what lets knockback exist. Distinct from *recovery*, which is about damage rather than agency. A source may declare one - a gun's `stunSeconds` (G0053) - and a man under it is **stunned**, drawn as its own state; everything else that holds a unit is a stagger, a lock or a throw. |
| **harm** | What a blow does to a man, declared by its source: **wound**, **down** or **kill**, named for what it does to a man with no wounds (G0053). Things with health read the blow's amount instead. |
| **wound** | One step of hurt on a man, and the harm that deals it. He carries up to his maximum and fights on, slower; one more puts him down. Several landing on one tick are one. |
| **wounded** | Standing with at least one wound. A description of a standing man, not a state of its own. |
| **grace** | The half second after a wound during which another wound cannot land. A **down** or a **kill** still can. A man's, where **recovery** is an alien's. |
| **shield** | Something that takes the next hit of any harm instead of him, and breaks. A commander comes back behind one that also runs out. |
| **mend** | Wounds clearing on their own after a spell with no new one. What *self-treatment* buys; nobody mends without it. |

### Driving a unit

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **aggro** | Whether a unit is hunting anybody. One latched boolean, and the whole of a unit's mind. *Who* it is hunting is not latched: that is whoever on the player's side is nearest and still on his feet, asked again every tick and published as a place (G0026). It notices at its detection range and, if its strain loses interest at all, gives up past its lose range. That man going down releases it regardless: nothing stands to attention over a corpse. |
| **aid** | A trooper leaving what it was doing to go and **stabilise** somebody. Ranked below a standing order and above the level's objectives, and taken on a **chance** rather than always (G0023). |
| **approach** | How wide of whatever it is hunting a creature closes. Each one walks at a point beside the man rather than at the man, on a side taken from where it was spawned rather than rolled (G0012's argument, applied to an approach) - which is what stops a pack converging onto one line and arriving in single file. The offset is spent by the time it is close, so the last of every approach is straight at him; a **spread** that held all the way in would be an orbit. A squad uses the same spread walking to a point and after the commander (G0041). |
| **rouse** | Sending everything within earshot of a place to go and see who is there. Two things shout: a **control point** somebody has started taking, and a **nest** somebody is hitting. Loud once per event rather than every tick, and a place rather than a target (G0016) - what it fights when it gets there is whatever is standing there. |
| **avoidance** | Steering around what a unit is about to hit, decided before it hits it. Context steering: a ring of candidate directions scored by how much the unit wanted to go that way against how dangerous that way is. It never avoids the other side, which is what it is trying to reach, and never a pickup it wants - only a **shunned** one gets room. |
| **chance** | How often a unit does something it only sometimes does, as an average per second of opportunity, dealt from a **due** rather than tossed every tick (G0039). Every one in the game is a constant in `AiChance` and a line in `assets/sim/*.properties`, so the conditional behaviour can be read off one list instead of found by searching the systems. Zero switches one off and draws no number at all. |
| **clearance** | Whether a body of a given size fits on a tile. Baked into a flow field rather than checked afterwards, which is what makes a one-tile crack a route for a grunt and a wall for a brute without anybody writing a rule about it. |
| **clear shot** | Whether a round fired from here would arrive there: a clear line through terrain, and nothing an **obstacle** would eat on the way. Not sight - a boulder hides nothing (G0015) - but a man who fires asks it before he picks a target, and so does a man on a gun. |
| **desired velocity** | Where a unit wants to be going this tick, before anything decides whether it may. Written by whatever is driving it, committed to a velocity by one system, and spent on the tick it was written - a unit nothing spoke for asks for nothing and stops. |
| **detection range** | How close the commander must come before an unaware unit notices him. |
| **field of view** | The arc a shooter will fire into, centred on where it is looking. Wider than a trooper's, so a creature built around a gun is harder to get behind than a man - and its aim **swings** onto a target at a rate rather than snapping, so stepping round one buys a real moment. |
| **swing** | A shooter bringing its aim round onto what it is hunting. A rate, not a snap: it is what turns a **field of view** from a number into something the player can use. |
| **hold ground** | A shooter that moves for somewhere to be and never for somebody to follow. It comes when summoned - a provocation is a place rather than a target, so taking a point off one brings it over - and it crosses the ground between noticing you and being able to fight you, once. From the moment it is **in position** it holds that ground however far you go, so you are driven off rather than chased. |
| **hop** | A **gait**: getting about in bursts with a sit between them rather than at a walk. It paces a course something else chose and never chooses one, and over one whole hop-and-sit it covers exactly what a walk would - so a hopping strain's `speed` means what every other strain's means. A clock rather than ground covered, which is the one place it differs from a **weave**: legs have a rhythm, and a creature pressed against a wall is still trying to hop. Its phase comes from where the unit was spawned, so two are out of step without a number being drawn. |
| **in position** | A shooter that has reached the distance it wanted. It outlives being in range on purpose: stepping out of its reach costs it the aim it had **settled**, and only losing it altogether lets it move up again. |
| **relative aim** | An aim sent as an offset from the commander rather than as a place on the field. What a stick has to send, because it knows a direction and nothing about where the commander is standing; a mouse only knows a place, so it sends one. `Simulation.step` is the one place that adds his position and turns the first into the second, so no system below it ever sees two kinds of aim. |
| **facing** | Which way a thing is looking, as a unit vector (`Facing`). Gameplay state: the arc a unit notices within is centred on it, and the renderer draws the same one. The commander's is his aim and never his heading (G0002); a trooper's is a decision of its own, so it can stand still and go on watching. Anything that does not steer itself has none. |
| **weave** | A creature refusing to walk in a straight line: its course is turned from side to side as it closes, so it arrives by a serpentine rather than along the line you were aiming down. A function of **ground covered** and of nothing else - not a clock, so being slowed does not change its shape, and not a roll (G0012), so it can be learnt and led. The phase comes from where the unit was spawned, which is what puts a pack out of step with itself without a number being drawn. Turns the course rather than adding to it, so a weaving creature is a harder shot and not a faster one. |
| **flow field** | A direction per tile, pointing the way to one goal, rebuilt when the goal leaves its tile. One grid pass serves every unit that reads it, however many there are - the alternative is a path search each and a queue walking in single file. |
| **forage** | An idle unit going to eat something it has noticed. Only ever reached by a unit that has not noticed the commander - he outranks food absolutely - which is why the secondary range is the shortest range in the game. |
| **intent** | What a commander is trying to do this tick, as a component. Written from input, read by systems. |
| **lose range** | How far he must get before a hunting unit gives up. Never shorter than the detection range - one number for both would flicker a unit standing exactly on the boundary between hunting and idling, every tick. |
| **patch** | The circle of ground an idle unit ambles around. Centred wherever it last had nothing to do: the anchor is dragged along behind anything hunting or feeding, so the moment a unit gives up it is already home. Anchoring to the spawn instead sent a unit that broke off a chase back across the level, which read as the level rewinding. |
| **scan** | A trooper sweeping its attention from side to side as it advances. The trooper's answer to *forage*: what it does with its attention while nothing is happening, and the reason its facing is worth drawing. |
| **sector** | A trooper's own run of the circle round a point he is standing on. The men on a point split the circle between them, each turning through his own a **scan** at a time, so nothing standing still round the point goes unseen. One man alone has all of it. |
| **alert** | A trooper who has been hit, for a while afterwards: he looks all round for what hit him rather than only inside his arc, and turns to face where it came from - a place, never who fired (G0016). Never through rock. |
| **threat** | What makes a trooper shoot one thing before another: something coming for somebody counts as nearer than something that is not, and each man already shooting at a thing makes it count as further, so a squad spreads its fire. A distance, re-chosen every tick, never a list he keeps. |
| **detour** | A trooper who has stopped getting anywhere backing off to one side for a moment - his own side first, the other side next time (G0042). What a stuck man does instead of planning again, because the route would say the same thing twice. |
| **secondary range** | How close something edible has to be before an idle unit goes for it. Shorter than the detection range on purpose: this is what a unit does with its attention when it has nothing else to do with it. |

### The commander's orders

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **earshot** | How far the commander's voice carries. The whole of who hears an order: there is no selection and no roster, so where he is standing is what decides. |
| **rally radius** | How near a point a commander has to be to count as being at it, for a squad holding the point before it (G0040). At the next point, the men set off at once; at their own, they stay with him. |
| **defence** | The order to hold here: stationary, still watching. Renamed from *stop* on 2026-09-13 - what a squad does when it stops is hold a place, and the verb should say so. |
| **forward** | The order to push on. Two things at once, deliberately: a burst of pace towards the objective, for a short distance rather than a countdown; and permission to leave a point the squad is holding and go and take the next one (G0029). Since G0040 a squad also goes on by itself to catch up with a commander who is **ahead** of it - never to run ahead of one. The second is what makes it the order that sets a level's pace - one press buys one point, and it is spent on arrival. It also **drops whatever standing order the man had**, because holding a place and following you are both reasons not to advance. Renamed from *move out* on 2026-09-13. |
| **order** | One thing the commander tells the troopers near him to do, as a standing instruction on each of them. Reaches only what is within earshot when it is given (G0017), outranks the level's objectives, and never interrupts a fight - which needs no queue, because nothing consumes it. **His death drops every standing order**, and none of them come back with him. |
| **urgency** | How much a trooper's advance beats its scanning, as a fraction. Deliberately **not** a speed - "forward" is what changes speed, and one dial doing both would make that order invisible. It sets how hard a man weaves on his way somewhere; since G0029 the pace of a *level* is the commander's, because the squad holds every point it takes until he says forward. |

### What the commander does with his hands

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **action** | Something the commander does to whatever is within arm's length, on one key. He never picks from a list: what is in reach is what is on offer, nearest first, and the simulation publishes which kind it is for the interface to show (G0021). |
| **crew** | To be aboard a **station** rather than standing on the ground. A man who is crewing one is driven by nobody, is out of the collision path entirely - nothing can shove, shoot or claw him - and is not drawn at all; what the player sees is the gun, which reads as manned. A **blast** still reaches him, which is what makes destroying a manned turret worth doing. Got on and off with the **action** key (G0024). |
| **station** | Something a man can **crew**. One exists - the **turret** - and a man aboard names it by its **handle**, exactly as a **load** is named (ADR 0016). |
| **haul** | To drag a load - a casualty or a barrel - along behind you. It costs almost everything: he backs away and cannot press forwards, keeps only a fraction of his pace and a little sideways lean, and has no hand free for a weapon. **Hauled** latches on the load, because what it buys is paid for after he puts it down. |
| **load** | Whatever is being hauled. Named by **handle** on both ends, so a drag that outlives what it was holding can never come back attached to something else (ADR 0016). |
| **reach** | How close something must be, surface to surface, for the commander to do anything with it. One number for every action there is, so there is one distance to learn. |

### The map and the view

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **arena** | The rectangle a level is played inside. Not a body - it is handled by clamping, not by a collision test. It is exactly the map's own extent; there is no separate arena size. |
| **focus** | A marker saying the view must keep this entity in shot. The simulation says who matters and stops there; where the camera goes is entirely the client's. |
| **ash** | Burnt ground. Passable; what is left where a place has already been fought over. |
| **fragment** | A piece of map that generation produces and assembles with others into a level. Not built yet; the grid exists to make it cheap. |
| **leash** | How far a commander may walk from the middle of the commanders. One shared view cannot frame four players free to walk apart for ever (pillar 4), so past it the outward part of a step is refused - never a shove inwards, so nobody is pushed and a man at the end of the rope walks home himself. A distance in world units, sized against the near-zoom view **by hand**: read from the camera it would turn pulling the view back into extra rope, and zoom is a rendering setting. |
| **mire** | Standing water and the mud round it. Passable; where the things that **hop** take root. |
| **region** | A contiguous area of a map, decided before its tiles are filled in. What makes a level read as places rather than one flat sheet: regions are shaded subtly apart, and later decide what terrain and what contents belong where. |
| **shake** | Something violent that happened somewhere, published for the view: how hard at the middle of it, how far it is felt, and how far through it is. The simulation says what happened and stops there - what the view does about it is entirely the client's, exactly as *focus* says who matters and not where the camera goes. |
| **spawn area** | The patch of tiles a side is put on at the start of a level. A rectangle of tiles with everyone jittered inside them, never at a tile centre (G0009) - the same tile-anchored scatter that places everything else. |
| **terrain** | The kind of ground on a tile. Names a role, like a `Look` does - what it looks like is the client's decision and what it costs a unit is a number in `assets/sim/*.properties`. Whether it can be stood on is the one exception, because generation needs it. |
| **thicket** | Dense undergrowth. Passable, and the only ground in the game worth going round: what standing on it costs is a **pace** rather than a route, so every flow field, reachability check and spawn treats it as the open ground it is. It **costs his side far more than theirs**, which is the whole of the mechanic and a declaration rather than a property of the ground - a thicket both sides waded equally would change how long the walk took and nothing else. Drawn as a hatch over the floor, because a thing that is going to cost you must not read as another colour of floor. |
| **tile** | One cell of the grid a level is laid out on. A unit of *construction*, not of play: nothing moves tile by tile and nothing is centred in one (G0009). |
| **variant** | Which of a look's several appearances an entity wears. Zero is the ordinary one. It exists for the commander: four identical circles is not a field anybody can read (pillar 5), so each player wears his own colour - and it is a number on `Appearance` rather than four looks in the enum, because the renderer decides what to draw from that component and asks nothing else about an entity (G0003). |
| **view** | What the camera shows of the field. On screen, the rectangle of the **letterbox** between the **top bar** and the **bottom bar**: the only place the fight is drawn. Its height is always `virtualHeight` world units; its width follows its shape, which is kept inside an aspect range, so a wider screen sees further to the sides. |

### Levels and objectives

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **redoubt** | The wide square the **Adventure** ends on: one **control point** in the middle of it, four **turrets** round that, and a **wave** schedule the level has been holding back all march. Taking the point is what starts the fight - a **post** goes down where it stands and the schedule is let go - so the player decides when the last fight begins. Content rather than machinery: what makes it a redoubt is the point that **holds afterwards** and nothing else. |
| **watch** | How far around a **post** has to be clear before it counts as held. Zero means the whole level, which is what a level the size of one fight means by it; a march names a distance instead, because "nothing hostile standing" cannot mean seven screens with **nests** still working four biomes back. Never the post's own radius - what you stand on to hold it is a few strides across, and what it is worth is measured by whatever is anywhere near it. |
| **post** | Ground a level says you must hold, and the first thing in the game that tells the **aliens** where to be: anything that has noticed nobody walks at it rather than ambling about its patch (G0027). It has no health, no side and no capture bar - it is a goal, not a thing to fight - and there is one per level. It may be put down mid-level rather than at the start, which is what a **control point** that **holds afterwards** does, and it names the **watch** it is measured over. Distinct from a **control point**, which is somewhere the squad goes and takes. |
| **nest** | A hole in the ground that keeps sending more of one **strain** out of it. The third way a level puts creatures on the field, after "everything is here from the first tick" and a **wave** schedule - and the only one that is a **place**, so it can be walked to, shot and taken away. It **sleeps** until somebody on the player's side comes near, and waking latches: backing off does not stop it. Destroying one empties the last **brood** out of it and **rouses** the neighbourhood, so a nest is at its worst the moment you kill it. |
| **brood** | One lot of creatures out of a **nest** - a few at a time, drawn between two numbers, jittered into the tiles around the hole like everything else (G0009). |
| **wave** | One lot of aliens, and when and where it arrives. A level may **hold the whole schedule back** until something arms it - taking the ground it then tells you to hold - so the last fight of a march is one the player starts. A list of them is carried by **the level entity**, and whether that list is spent is half of the "hold the post" objective: an empty field means you have won if there is nothing left to come, and means you are between waves if there is. |
| **lair** | What a level keeps off the field until every commander on his feet is standing on one patch of ground, and that ground. Level state beside the **wave** schedule (G0007); it wakes once, and puts down everything it holds - a **boss**, its **nests** and whatever **pickups** lie round it - on that tick (G0048). Nothing of it exists before then, so pulling the **view** back shows nothing waiting. |
| **control point** | Ground a squad takes by standing near it for a while. Captured by dwelling; the dwell goes back to zero the moment the last capturer leaves, rather than pausing, and starting an attempt wakes what is nearby. |
| **level** *(source)* | One self-contained combat scenario, start to objective. |
| **objective** | A condition a level requires before it counts as done. A level names a set and requires all of it. Two exist: clear the map of enemies, and capture every control point. What a completed level *does* is still open - in the alpha it does nothing. |
| **proving ground** | The menu's training mode: pick a strain and a gun and fight it. A placeholder page for now. Not *arena*, which is the rectangle a level is played inside. |
| **scenario** | One arena the alpha-phase menu can launch, built to make a system visible. Content, never design. |
| **the level entity** | One entity, spawned with the world and never removed, carrying state that belongs to the level rather than to anything standing in it (G0007). |

### Upgrades

What the player's side earns on the field and holds for the rest of the level, and what the aliens
take in answer (ADR 0017, G0049). The words `docs/design/upgrades-scope.md` settled; the code arrives
from S2.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **adaptation** | An **upgrade** the aliens take, on a schedule and with no **chooser**. Not *evolve*, which stays one alien changing strain by feeding. |
| **armoury deck** | The weighted cards a **sealed cache** is dealt from, one deck per level, drawn so no gun comes up twice before every card has (G0050). An upgrade may add cards. |
| **away** | A slot whose machine's link has dropped. Its commander stands idle, the **rotation** skips it, and its **drafts** pass on (G0052). Only the host's sequencer says so; offline nobody is ever away. |
| **capability** | Behaviour written once and carried from spawn by every unit that could use it, off at zero until an upgrade turns it up - a trooper's grenade throw (G0051). How an upgrade unlocks something without adding a component. |
| **card** | One **upgrade** on a **draft**, dealt when the draft is earned. |
| **chooser** | The slot a **draft** belongs to. |
| **draft** | One offer of up to three **upgrades** to one player, earned by an objective: a point taken, a hold wave cleared, a boss killed. He takes one card, when he likes; nothing expires. |
| **draft bar** | The top bar while a **draft** is open: the **chooser** on the left, his **cards** across the rest. One per machine, driven by the seat that opened it; it pauses a solo game. |
| **effect** | One change an upgrade makes: scale a **stat**, add to a count, raise a chance, scale one gun, or add cards to the **armoury deck**. Each names its **target**. |
| **ledger** | What each side holds, with **ranks** - level state on the level entity (G0007). Numbers are folded from it where they are read, never written back. |
| **pending tell** | `UPGRADE x2 C` at the end of a player's panel's first row: his slot holds **drafts**, and the key that opens them. |
| **pick** | Taking a **card**: an input event naming the draft and the upgrade, never the card's position. |
| **rank** | How many times one upgrade has been taken (I, II, III). Each rank applies its effects once more. |
| **rotation** | The order **drafts** go round the present players: ascending slot order, starting after the last **chooser**, skipping anyone **away**. |
| **shared** / **personal** | Whether an upgrade's **ranks** belong to the side or to the slot that took it. It is personal when every effect targets the **chooser**. |
| **stat** | A number an **effect** may move. Read in exactly one place; a stat nobody reads is a config key that lies. |
| **target** | Whom an **effect** reaches: the squad, the commanders, the chooser's commander, the team (squad and commanders), the side (numbers no entity reads - men per crate, headroom, the deck), or the aliens. A turret is never a target (G0024). |
| **upgrade** | A permanent improvement the player's side holds for the rest of the level, earned from a **draft**. Kinds are `UpgradeKind`; numbers are `sim.upgrade.<kind>.*`. |

### Online play

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **build id** | A hash of every source that decides what a tick does - `map`, `sim` and `net` - generated by the build. Two **peers** with different ones are refused at the handshake; it is the one mismatch a **session** cannot heal (ADR 0012). |
| **cosmetic lead** | Drawing a machine's own commanders facing where their players aim this frame, rather than where the world has them a **lead** of ticks later. Drawn and nothing else: shots still go where the world says. Possible because a commander's facing is the direction to its aim, with no turn rate between (ADR 0012, G0002). |
| **desync dump** | What one machine knew about a tick the peers' worlds disagreed on: its **keyframe** at that tick, the last agreed one, and every **tick bundle** between. The diverged peer and the **host** each write one beside the connection log; `DesyncReport` reads the pair (ADR 0012). |
| **drop-in** | Joining a game already under way, at any point in any level, by plugging a pad in or by another machine connecting. There is no lobby and nobody waits for anybody (G0028, G0038). |
| **host** | The **peer** that started the **session**. Its machine runs the **sequencer** and its config is the one every peer plays on (ADR 0012). The session ends when it leaves - for now: the sequencer holds no world, so it can move. |
| **instance** | One copy of the game running on a PC, numbered by `--instance` when more than one runs - its window, its connection log, its pads. Not a **peer**: a peer is a place in a session, and an instance on the menu is in none. |
| **input delay** | How many ticks pass between a player pressing something and the tick that uses it: the **lead** of his machine. Two on the host and about four to six on a peer in the same region - the price of lockstep, and the number pillar 1 judges it by. |
| **input event** | A one-off input - an order given, a **pick**, and every one-off press to come. On the wire each is a number, a slot, a type and its value; the **sequencer** seals at most one of each type per slot per tick. Numbered and sent again until the **sequencer** has it, then applied once; never repeated for a late peer and never lost to one. Everything else a player does is held input, which a late peer's slot repeats from its previous tick (ADR 0012). |
| **keyframe** | A **snapshot** a peer keeps every `checksumTicks`, from the same write that makes its checksum - what a **desync dump** is made of. |
| **lead** | How many ticks ahead of the tick it is playing a **peer** tags its input, so the input reaches the **sequencer** before that tick is sealed. Adapted to the round trip as the sequencer reports how early input arrives - judged only on input sent while the peer was in step, never while it catches up - and it is that peer's **input delay** (ADR 0012). |
| **loading** | A **peer** that has been sent a **snapshot** and not yet built it. The **sequencer** sends it no **tick bundles** until its first input about that world arrives, then every tick since; one still loading when those ticks leave the history is let go and told why (ADR 0012). |
| **link** | This machine's connection to one other, as the transport sees it: a number, carrying reliable and unreliable messages, opened once both can cross it and closed exactly once, with a reason (ADR 0012). A **peer** is who is at the other end; a link is how they are reached. |
| **peer** | One machine in a **session**, with up to four **seats** on it. Every peer runs the whole simulation. |
| **sequencer** | What decides which input applies to each tick: it collects every peer's, seals each tick when its time comes and sends out the **tick bundle**. It runs on a thread of its own and holds no simulation, so a stalled window on the host's machine stalls only that window (ADR 0012). |
| **room code** | Four characters that name a **session** - read out to a friend, typed on his join screen. Nothing in it reads as anything else (no 0/O, 1/I/L, 2/Z, 5/S, 8/B). It names a session and grants nothing (G0038). |
| **session** | One game shared by one or more **peers**, from the host starting it to the host leaving. An `OnlineSession` is what one machine holds of it; offline, a `ScenarioSession` is the whole game. |
| **snapshot** | The whole simulation written down at the end of one tick - every entity, component, stream and counter - so that another machine can carry on from it. What a **drop-in** receives, what a peer that has desynced is healed with, and what a desync dump starts from. Written from the components' own fields (ADR 0014). |
| **tick bundle** | Every slot's input for one tick, as the **sequencer** sealed it - held input, **input events**, and the session's own restart or god mode switch. A peer steps a tick only once it holds that tick's bundle. |

### The interface

What the player is shown around the fight and how he moves through it. Decided in the `ui` module
(ADR 0015); the page-by-page specification is `docs/ui/menu_layout_agent.md`.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **bottom bar** | The black band under the **view**: four **player panels**, or the **roll call**. Its height is derived from what it holds, never configured. |
| **dispatch** | One line the **terminal** has been told to say: what kind of news, about which player, with up to two numbers. A kind is a constant and a template in `ui.properties`, not a class per event. |
| **entry** | One thing on a **page** or **modal** that can be selected and confirmed - a scenario in a list, RESUME. Not *item*, which is a pickup's banned word. |
| **letterbox** | The screen's split into a **top bar**, the **view** and a **bottom bar**, with black pillars beside the view when the screen is wider than the view's aspect range allows. The fight is only ever in the view; the interface is only ever in the bars. |
| **modal** | A **page** drawn over whatever is behind it rather than instead of it - the pause menu over the level, the exit confirm over the main menu. It takes the input until it closes. |
| **modality** | Which kind of device the player last used meaningfully - pointer, or keys and pads. It decides how the **selection** is drawn, whether the cursor shows, and which button names the hints use. |
| **page** | One screenful of the menu - the main page, the campaign list, the online page. Pages are a stack inside one libGDX `Screen`: going in pushes, Back pops. Never called a *screen*, which is the libGDX class that holds them. |
| **paused banner** | One line over the top of the **view** saying the game is paused and which key opens the menu, shown when it is paused and no menu is open on this machine - somebody else paused, or the harness's `P`. Takes no input. |
| **player panel** | One quarter of the **bottom bar**, belonging to one player and drawn in his colour: his label, **status**, gun and heat, and later his portrait and tally. While a game has four players or fewer, local and online alike, every one of them has a panel; with more, only this machine's seats do. A player keeps his quarter while he is in the game, so nobody's panel moves when somebody joins. Grey, and saying OFFLINE, while his pad or his link is gone. |
| **readout** | The facts the interface shows, copied out of the simulation once a frame by the one class that reads `sim` for it. Everything in the bars is drawn from it, and nothing on screen is a number the readout does not carry (pillar 5). |
| **reporter** | A small client object that compares this frame's **readout** with the last one and files a **dispatch** for one kind of fact that changed - points taken, waves sent, a commander down. The simulation announces nothing (G0004); reporters notice. A new world is taken silently, so a player dropping in mid-level is not told the whole history. |
| **reticle** | The game's own aim mark, in a player's colour, over everything: the keyboard player's on the mouse, hiding the system pointer; a pad player's a fixed distance along his aim. Each is a setting, off by default. |
| **roll call** | The **bottom bar**'s other view: one compact row per player - colour, **status**, tally - for games of more than four, where **player panels** cannot hold everybody. Swapped with the panels by a key. A placeholder for now. Not *roster*, which G0017 rules out for the squad. |
| **scrim** | The dark veil drawn over the **view** while a menu is open, dim enough to say the world is not live and light enough to read the frozen fight under it. |
| **selection** | Which **entry** of a **page** confirming would act on, drawn as a ring or a hover. Each page remembers its own. Not *focus*, which is who the camera keeps in shot. |
| **status** | The first line of a **player panel** after his label: empty while he stands, and otherwise the one word that matters - down (with his bleed-out), KIA (with the seconds to his return) or aboard. Not *health*: a commander has none worth a bar - what reaches him either downs him or kills him. |
| **tally** | What one commander has done in this life - enemies killed, control points taken while he stood on them, men put back on their feet - carried on him and reaped with him (G0044). A kill is credited to the slot its killing blow carried. Shown in his **player panel** as three icons; never spent. Not *score*, which would imply a total and something to spend it on. |
| **terminal** | The **top bar**'s in-game terminal under the goal - the battlefield's voice, not a log: the last few **dispatches**, the newest typed out at the bottom and the older dimmed above it. Narrative only, never technical, never repeating the goal line or a panel. An alarm - a commander down - appears at once; stale news is dropped unshown; nothing fades on a timer. |
| **top bar** | The black band over the **view**: the mission, the current goal and the **terminal** on the left, the dev overlay on the right. Its height is derived from what it holds. |
| **UI canvas** | The pixel grid the interface is drawn on: `canvasHeight` UI pixels tall, each one a whole number of screen pixels square. Nothing on it is scaled by a fraction, so text and frames are always crisp. The world is not on it - the camera zooms smoothly. |

### Time, space and randomness

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **alpha** | Fraction of a tick elapsed, used to interpolate rendering between ticks. |
| **bag** | A range of values dealt without replacement: every one comes up once before any comes up again. The whole of one is a `long`, one bit per value (`map.Bag`). What a nest's brood sizes and a level's crates are drawn from. |
| **due** | What a unit still has to spend before an occasional behaviour next happens: a budget averaging one, drawn after every occurrence and spent down by each tick of opportunity (G0039). Why a **chance** is dealt rather than tossed. |
| **frame** | One rendered image. Zero or more ticks happen per frame. Never a tick. |
| **handle** | Which entity this is, for anything that has to find it again later: a `long` issued once when it is spawned and never reused, so holding one across ticks is always safe - it finds the entity or nobody. How a component names another entity; never by the `Entity` object, which the engine recycles (ADR 0016). |
| **longest gap** | The longest a unit ever waits for a behaviour it has the opportunity for, as a multiple of the average wait. Zero means no limit - a coin tossed every tick. One number for every **chance** (G0039). |
| **stream** | One seeded generator per concern, all derived from the level seed. Sharing one sequence means re-tuning how often anything draws a number reshuffles every number every other system gets - reproducible, and miserable to iterate on. |
| **tick** | One fixed simulation step, `SimConfig.tickSeconds()` long. Never a frame. Every timer counts whole ticks, converted once from the seconds the config writes (`Ticks`). |
| **world unit** | The simulation's unit of length. Never pixels. |

## Words we do not use

- *unit* alone - ambiguous between trooper, entity and world unit. Say which.
- *type* or *class* for what a creature is - say strain. *Kind* is fine for the enum in code
  (`UnitKind`, `PickupKind`), but prose says strain.
- *item*, *collectable*, *powerup* - say pickup.
- *mutation*, *transformation* - say evolve.
- *evolve* for the aliens' side-wide change - say adaptation. Evolve is one alien changing strain.
- *buff*, *perk*, *skill*, *talent* - say upgrade. *Power-up* already means a pickup here.
- *offer*, *choice*, *level-up* for what an objective earns - say draft; for one option on it, card.
- *object*, *actor*, *thing* - say entity.
- *barrel* for the end of a gun - say muzzle. A barrel is the explosive.
- *ammo*, *ammunition*, *reload* for what heat does - say heat and overheat. Heat is a rate limit and
  not a supply, and grenades are the only thing a count is still open for.
- *mount*, *emplacement*, *vehicle* - say turret for the thing and station for the role.
- *enter*, *board*, *ride* - say crew, or "aboard" for the state.
- *update* for a simulation step - say tick.
- *client* or *server* for a machine in a session - say peer, or host. *Client* already means the
  `core` module.
- *lobby* or *match* for a game other machines can join - say session.
- *create* or *make* for bringing an entity into the world - say spawn.
- *focus* for the entry a menu would act on - say selection. Focus is who the camera keeps in shot.
- *screen* for one screenful of the menu - say page. A `Screen` is the libGDX class that holds pages.
- *arena* for the training mode - say proving ground.
- *health bar* for a commander - say status. He has no health worth a bar.
- *HP*, *hit points*, *health* for a commander or a trooper - say wounds. A man has none (G0053).
- *i-frames*, *invincibility* for a man - say grace, or shield. *Invulnerable* is the harness's god mode.
- *small* or *light damage*, *lethal damage* - say the harm: wound, down or kill.
